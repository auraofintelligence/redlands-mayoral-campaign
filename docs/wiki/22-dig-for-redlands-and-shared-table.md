# 22 Dig for Redlands and Shared Table

[Wiki home](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home) · [Words and names](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home#words-and-names) · Food, repair and community wealth

An idea by [Luke Nathan Hayes](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/redlands-mayoral-campaign/about.html), an Amity resident on Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island working on digital tools, community proposals and music.

Luke Nathan Hayes decided on 10 November 2026 not to contest the 2026 Redland City mayoral by-election. This Wiki explores civic questions and possible project adaptations.

## The idea

Dig for Redlands and Shared Table aim to help people grow and share healthy local produce, with the practical goal of easing household food costs.

## Words and project names

- **Community Hours (C-hours)**: A way to recognise verified voluntary human contribution and its outcomes. The source introduction describes one verified hour of contribution with no monetary equivalent. Participation and information sharing are choices.

## What this could mean for Redlands

Dig for Australia-style growing could be adapted to sunny balconies, fences, yards, schools and suitable shared land in Redlands. Local crop guidance, seed and seedling swaps, and optional produce-sharing circles would support participation.

## Projects behind this idea

These introductions describe the source projects. The Redlands proposal above explains the possible local adaptation.

- **[Straddie Shared Table](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/shared-table-initiative/)**. A food-resilience and sharing workbench covering gardens, pantries, freezers, preserving and safe shared meals under fuel and freight pressure. It includes food-circle planning, Garden Mate growing prompts and Kitchen Mate food-audit and meal-planning prompts.

- **[C-Hour Introduction](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/C-Hour-introduction/)**. An introduction and reference library for Community Hours: one verified hour of voluntary human contribution, with no monetary equivalent. It explores locally chosen recognition, reciprocity, personal intelligence and shared capability.

## How this could move forward

Community partnership

## What the mayor and council could do

The mayor could convene growers and willing hosts and seek council support for food-growing information, suitable access to council land or venues, and transparent community grant opportunities.

## Who else makes decisions

Landholders and participating bodies corporate choose access to their spaces. Food-sharing hosts and growers retain responsibility for their own activities and applicable requirements.

## People and organisations who could be involved

Residents, renters/owners, bodies corporate, schools, growers, garden groups and suitable landholders.

## How related interests would be handled

Seed, equipment, land-access and grant decisions would need transparent processes. Any specific council benefit to Luke, Strange But True or an organisation he joined would require independent handling.
