# 50 Power, materials and circular capacity for Redlands

[Wiki home](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home) · [Words and names](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home#words-and-names) · Redlands sovereign capacity and civic establishments

An idea by [Luke Nathan Hayes](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/redlands-mayoral-campaign/about.html), an Amity resident on Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island working on digital tools, community proposals and music.

## The idea

Redlands projects could plan the electricity, water, materials, equipment and repair services they need together, with clear accounts of costs, use and recovery.

## Words and project names

- **Sovereign capacity / sovereign compute**: The practical ability to provide and govern useful computing, skills, equipment and services, including decisions about where data is kept and how systems are run.

## A practical example

A resource plan is like a project’s working list of supplies and running costs: what is needed, how much, when it is available, who maintains it and what happens at the end of its working life.

## What this could mean for Redlands

A resource plan would connect each service’s work with its equipment, electricity, cooling, water, networks, materials, spare parts, replacement and recovery needs. Service owners and suppliers could record quantities, stages, responsibilities and opportunities to share or reuse resources.

## What it needs to work

Start with the useful service, then trace its equipment back through factory materials, recycling and mining. Record what reaches the finished product, manufacturing losses, recycled and newly mined supply, ore quality and recovery. Keep the rate of electricity use, total energy over time and reliable supply when needed as separate measures. Record storage duration and losses, network connections and when equipment is actually ready to use. Protect electricity needed for the clean-energy transition and essential development, with extra demand shown clearly. Count operation, maintenance, repair and recovery across the equipment’s working life.

## How this could move forward

Council, regional, state, federal and business cooperation

## What the mayor and council could do

Council can examine its own facilities, waste/resource recovery, water/services, infrastructure planning and purchasing, and convene utilities, industry, researchers and other governments around the wider requirements.

## Who else makes decisions

Electricity networks, generation, major mineral extraction/refining, industrial capacity and relevant environmental approvals involve responsible operators and state and federal authorities. Each local use still needs the appropriate land, service and operating arrangements.

## People and organisations who could be involved

Council asset, water and waste teams, energy/network providers, material processors, repair/reuse businesses, engineers, researchers, landholders and state and federal infrastructure partners.

## How related interests would be handled

Relevant interests include a supplier, asset or financial interest in the energy, materials, equipment, maintenance or recovery arrangements recorded in the resource plan.
