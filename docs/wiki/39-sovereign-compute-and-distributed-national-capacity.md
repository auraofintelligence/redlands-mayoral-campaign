# 39 Sovereign compute and distributed national capacity

[Wiki home](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home) · [Words and names](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home#words-and-names) · Advocacy and research context

An idea by [Luke Nathan Hayes](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/redlands-mayoral-campaign/about.html), an Amity resident on Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island working on digital tools, community proposals and music.

## The idea

Australian and locally governed computing could support personal AI, community projects, public services and useful production, with clear choices about where information is kept.

## Words and project names

- **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: Computer systems that can generate or analyse material such as text, images and data. Their output needs checking against the purpose and the source.
- **Sovereign capacity / sovereign compute**: The practical ability to provide and govern useful computing, skills, equipment and services, including decisions about where data is kept and how systems are run.
- **The missing middle**: The people, places, equipment and ongoing work needed between an idea and a service people can actually use. The linked project develops a plan for locally owned community computing.

## A practical example

Like a shared workshop, a computing service needs suitable equipment, reliable power, people who can maintain it and clear access arrangements.

## What this could mean for Redlands

A Redlands computing plan would explain the jobs the equipment needs to do, the case for investment and who could use the service. It would cover processing and storage, electricity, cooling, networks, access, reliability, running costs and responsibility. Plans would also explain how information and services could be moved or recovered if a provider changed.

## Projects behind this idea

These introductions describe the source projects. The Redlands proposal above explains the possible local adaptation.

- **[Sovereign Capacity Lab](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/sovereign-capacity-lab/)**. A browser-based research and scenario laboratory for mining, materials, power, repairable robots and locally governed infrastructure. Its workbenches connect physical resource accounts, distributed compute, manufacturing, Australian industry and a 2032 planning horizon.

- **[Installing the Missing Middle](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/installing-the-missing-middle/)**. A national installation-business plan for a federation of locally owned community-compute nodes. It combines computing, useful heat, backup power, installation learning, shared ownership, finance and hardware renewal.

- **[Aura Direct Hardware](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/aura-direct-hardware/)**. Research into local-first hardware, custom silicon and a decentralised compute commons for Aura of Intelligence. Its pathways span XR wearables, food computers, industrial processing, place-aware infrastructure, agent design and a wider Space Web.

## How this could move forward

Advocacy and work with other governments

## What the mayor and council could do

The mayor could convene prospective public users, hosts, providers and regional partners, bring council service requirements into the design, and seek appropriate council decisions and external investment through the funding route in entry 12.

## Who else makes decisions

Infrastructure owners and providers decide their facilities and service agreements. Power/network owners and external funding or regulatory bodies retain their responsibilities.

## People and organisations who could be involved

Universities, compute and energy providers, neighbouring councils, community hosts and responsible state and federal agencies.

## How related interests would be handled

Relevant interests include ownership, licence income or a paid role connected to a compute purchase, hosting contract, facility agreement or capacity provider considered by council.
