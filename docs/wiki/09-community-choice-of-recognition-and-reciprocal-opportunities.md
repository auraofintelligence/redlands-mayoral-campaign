# 09 Community choice of recognition and reciprocal opportunities

[Wiki home](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home) · [Words and names](https://github.com/auraofintelligence/redlands-mayoral-campaign/wiki/Home#words-and-names) · Community contribution and reciprocity

An idea by [Luke Nathan Hayes](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/redlands-mayoral-campaign/about.html), an Amity resident on Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island working on digital tools, community proposals and music.

## The idea

Residents and hosts could choose meaningful ways to recognise contributions, based on what people value and what the community can offer.

## Words and project names

- **Community Hours (C-hours)**: A way to recognise verified voluntary human contribution and its outcomes. The source introduction describes one verified hour of contribution with no monetary equivalent. Participation and information sharing are choices.

## What this could mean for Redlands

Residents and hosts could discuss shared meals, skill exchanges, tool access, learning, events and other affordable ways to say thanks. Costs, donated resources and fair access rules would be set out before choosing trials.

## Projects behind this idea

These introductions describe the source projects. The Redlands proposal above explains the possible local adaptation.

- **[C-Hour Introduction](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/C-Hour-introduction/)**. An introduction and reference library for Community Hours: one verified hour of voluntary human contribution, with no monetary equivalent. It explores locally chosen recognition, reciprocity, personal intelligence and shared capability.

- **[Community Club Builder: Sandy Sports](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/community-club-builder-sandy-sports/)**. A practical playbook for forming and running a community club, using a Minjerribah sand-sports club as its worked example. It covers governance, inclusive leadership, membership, shared administration, AI assistance and funding preparation.

## How this could move forward

Community partnership

## What the mayor and council could do

The mayor could bring residents, hosts and businesses together to discuss recognition they would value or offer. Any council contribution would follow its own decision process.

## Who else makes decisions

Hosts, businesses and community organisations decide their own offers and capacity; the treatment of a particular benefit depends on its actual arrangements.

## People and organisations who could be involved

Participants, community hosts, local businesses, volunteer organisations and relevant legal and tax advisers.

## How related interests would be handled

Luke’s tools, equipment or provider role would be identified if council considered funding a reciprocal offer or selecting him to supply it.
